# Safewater Lines — final analysis synthesis for presentation planning

Prepared 29 October 2026. The primary focus on cross-team execution and fairness was selected by the user. This consolidates the analysis; presentation structure and storyline will be decided next.

## Scope and confidence

327 supplied master response records; 30 complete rating items; 1,892 nonblank comments from 321 records. Response rate and workforce representativeness are unknown. Quantitative statements describe these records. Initial qualitative coding has a documented same-assistant re-review of 568 comments, including all 104 original flags; 53 decisions were revised and 75 cases still need context confirmation. Independent human validation has not been performed. Requests are not incident counts, dissatisfaction diagnoses or causal effects.

## Core interpretation

Safewater Lines has a positive foundation of pride and mutual support. The practical opportunity is to make cross-team execution and people decisions more dependable, explainable and consistent, supported by recognition, role clarity/backup and visible follow-through. This is an evidence-based working interpretation, not a proven causal model.

## Preserve the foundation

Company pride PRI1 is 92.4% favourable, contribution clarity PRI2 95.7%, and within-team support CAM1 91.1%. In the preserve prompt, 119 records identify team/colleague-related strengths. Protect these strengths while addressing gaps; do not generalise one experience to everyone.

## P1: Cross-team execution (Primary user-selected focus)

**Ratings:** CAM1 91.1% vs CAM2 82.3% favourable; CAM4 80.1%; paired discordance 45 vs 16 records.

**Comments:** 53 teamwork answers discuss roles/processes; communication–teamwork co-mentions 98 answers, strongly prompt-dependent.

**Proposed action:** Test handover ownership, clear response/escalation expectations and backup coverage.

**Proposed owner:** Functional heads with Operations and HR.

**Baseline and measures:** Pilot one workflow; record handover delays, rework and unresolved escalations before/after; repeat CAM2/CAM4 wording.

**Limit:** Descriptive differences; no evidence identifying a specific department pair or causal process.

## P2: Fairness of people decisions (Primary user-selected focus)

**Ratings:** FAI3 74.6%, FAI5 75.5%, FAI1 76.1% favourable; 137 records have at least one FAI item at 1–3.

**Comments:** Fairness prompt: 67 career/learning, 66 recognition and 64 communication requests; 73 no-concern replies.

**Proposed action:** Clarify and consistently apply appraisal, progression and reward criteria; provide reasons and a review route.

**Proposed owner:** HR and business leadership.

**Baseline and measures:** Baseline FAI1/FAI3/FAI5; review criteria coverage, employee understanding and procedural consistency.

**Limit:** 1–3 includes Sometimes; no universal unfairness or discrimination finding; mixed experiences retained.

## S1: Recognition and feedback (Supporting theme)

**Ratings:** RES4 72.5% and CAM5 72.8% favourable.

**Comments:** 179 unique records request recognition/feedback; fairness–recognition pair support 57 answers / 53 records.

**Proposed action:** Improve regular, specific feedback and visible, explainable recognition across roles.

**Proposed owner:** People managers and HR.

**Baseline and measures:** Baseline RES4/CAM5; track access to recognition and quality/usefulness of feedback.

**Limit:** Topic includes performance feedback, not only awards; do not assume gifts resolve perceived fairness.

## S2: Role clarity, workload and backup (Operational enabler)

**Ratings:** FAI4 workload fairness 83.2% favourable, with 36 Sometimes and 19 unfavourable responses.

**Comments:** 91 records request roles/process changes; 78 request workload/boundary changes; counts overlap.

**Proposed action:** Document responsibilities, backup, priority-setting and expectations for non-working-time contact.

**Proposed owner:** Functional managers with HR.

**Baseline and measures:** Baseline FAI4; establish operational backup/after-hours measures and review whether staff can use the arrangements.

**Limit:** No validated burnout diagnosis or workload-volume measure; specific accounts require verification.

## S3: Voice and visible follow-through (Leadership enabler)

**Ratings:** CRE5 79.8% favourable; 80/327 choose HR Communication as their one priority.

**Comments:** 134 records request voice/action and 194 communication/explanations; overlapping prompted topics.

**Proposed action:** Create an accountable concern-to-action loop with acknowledgement, owner, progress and closure feedback.

**Proposed owner:** Leadership and HR with relevant action owners.

**Baseline and measures:** Baseline CRE5; establish acknowledgement/closure times and ask whether employees saw useful change.

**Limit:** Communication frequency alone is not proof of listening or problem resolution.

## Depth behind the two main priorities

Cross-team execution: 45/327 records (13.8%) are favourable on CAM1 but at 1–3 on CAM2; 16 show the reverse. On the original categories, 90 rate CAM1 higher, 203 the same and 34 lower. Since identical-category respondents cannot be discordant, retaining the count of 45 after excluding them is not an independent robustness check. We do not know which specific department pairs or operational steps cause delays.

Fairness: 137/327 records (41.9%) have at least one fairness item at 1–3; 97 have two or more. Sometimes is included, so this is not the prevalence of unfair treatment. 51 rate both decision consistency (FAI3) and understanding decisions (FAI5) at 1–3. Cross-team and fairness patterns overlap for 47 records, but 90 records have a fairness item at 1–3 despite favourable CAM2; the priorities are related but not interchangeable.

Group profiles are descriptive follow-up, not causal explanations or blame. The 3–5-year group contains 53 responses; 11 show the CAM1-favourable/CAM2-1–3 pattern and 27 have at least one fairness item at 1–3. Customer Service has 51 responses, with corresponding counts of 10 and 29. Composition and selection can influence these patterns; Other is a heterogeneous grouping.

## What the VOSviewer-style map contributes

The primary map uses 1230 topic-coded suggestion/mixed answers from 305 response records and 13 displayed topics. A link counts topics in the same answer. Node size shows unique record support, line width answer co-mentions, and colors custom exploratory communities. Our local layout/clustering is not native VOSviewer software.

Communication/teamwork: 98 answers from 93 records, of which 85 answers come from the teamwork prompt. Fairness/career: 46 answers from 43 records, of which 41 come from the fairness prompt. The interactive question-exclusion control exposes this prompt dependence. The network helps organise discussion; it is not independent validation, a causal map or a severity ranking.

## Verification track alongside the priorities

Specific accounts/requested changes about workplace conditions, equipment, respectful correction and time boundaries should be checked confidentially. They are unverified employee accounts. Broad topic counts do not establish the frequency of serious incidents. Remove names and distinctive details from presentation examples; no raw quotations are included here.

## Requirements retained for the presentation discussion

The final presentation should preserve the strength story, establish the cross-team and fairness evidence, explain the three supporting mechanisms, communicate limitations, and connect selected interventions to proposed ownership, implementation sequence and success measures. Network graphics should remain explanatory rather than dominate the story. Detailed slide structure, visual theme, speaking roles and storyline have not been fixed in this stage.

The action options are concrete proposals, not organisational commitments. Step 6 now supplies the 0–90-day proposed roadmap, KPI definitions and proposed pilot operating standards. Outcome target-setting gates and owner/resource approval remain explicit organisational decisions. No new fuzzy, causal driver, employee clustering or attrition-prediction model is needed to make these recommendations defensible.
