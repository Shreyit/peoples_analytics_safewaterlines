# Presentation storyline and speaker guide

16 main slides, followed by 4 reference slides. Allow roughly 15 minutes for the main story (about a minute per slide); use the appendix for questions. Timing is a suggested rehearsal plan, not an assignment requirement.

All suggested owners, standards and pilot actions require organisational decisions. Quotes are verbatim, de-identified and chosen as typical examples, not as evidence of frequency.

## 01 · Proud people. Fairer decisions. A listening loop that closes.

Open with the organisational question: what should Safewater Lines protect, and what should it fix first? People are proud and feel they belong. The gaps are in how people decisions are made and explained, and in whether leaders visibly act on what employees raise. We propose one 90-day pilot with three workstreams, measured with an anonymous pulse. Use the dashboard for questions rather than walking through every metric.

**Evidence:** Your Voice master survey; analysis snapshot 30 October 2026.

## 02 · Protect pride. Make people decisions fair and visible. Close the listening loop.

Lead with the recommendation. Protect what works: pride, belonging and own-team support are all above 90% favourable. Priority 1 is fair, explainable people decisions, growth and recognition: the five lowest-scoring questions sit here, and Fairness is the block most strongly linked to recommending, satisfaction and intention to stay (31% of the explained variation). Priority 2 is leaders listening and visibly acting: it is the top actionable link to outcomes, the most common HR request and the most raised 'one change'. Cross-team handovers show a real but narrower gap; fix them in a targeted pilot starting in Customer Service. All links are associations from one survey, not proven causes.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv; priority_matrix.csv; priority_triangulation.csv; driver_model_summary.csv; group_hotspots.csv.

## 03 · Positive overall, and noticeably less so when people stay anonymous

Most respondents intend to stay and would recommend Safewater Lines. But 69% typed their name in the optional field, and the 100 anonymous respondents are clearly less positive: 78% intend to stay for 3–5 years versus 92% of named respondents. This is consistent with people being more guarded when named, although who chose to give a name may also differ. Either way, report both figures and run the next survey without a name field. The right-hand column gathers the reading rules in one place so the rest of the deck can stay direct.

**Evidence:** named_vs_anonymous.csv; item_evidence_profile.csv; survey_clean.csv; notebook Step 7.4.

## 04 · Pride and helpful colleagues are assets to protect

Three items show a strong foundation: 95.7% understand their contribution to business success, 92.4% are proud to work for Safewater Lines and 91.1% report help within their own team. In the 'what should we never lose' question, 119 respondents name their team or colleagues. Pride items are among the strongest associations with recommending and intending to stay (Spearman about 0.5–0.6), so protecting them matters for retention, not only for morale. Own-team support is high but only weakly related to the outcomes. Use all three as guardrails: the pilot should not erode them.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv (PRI2, PRI1, CAM1); employee_voice_topic_summary.csv (preserve topic t); notebook §8.

## 05 · Fairness is the weakest survey block

Pride has the highest favourable share and Fairness the lowest. Respect and Camaraderie sit below Credibility and Overall experience. Each block has five complete items and 1,635 valid answers. The block labels come from the questionnaire and are not proven to be statistically distinct, so the next slides work at the level of individual questions. Do not compare these figures with an external benchmark or an official Trust Index; there is no valid matching basis.

**Evidence:** survey_clean.csv; five items per block; 1,635 valid answers per block; equal-weight working means.

## 06 · Six questions matter most and score below the median: fix these first

The vertical axis shows how much of the variation in recommending, satisfaction and intention to stay each question accounts for, using relative-weights analysis, which handles correlated questions fairly. Together the 19 questions explain 65% of that variation. The horizontal axis is today's favourable share. The shaded corner holds questions that matter more than average and score below the median: leaders acting on feedback, equal growth opportunities, teams working together, fair rewards, consistent decisions and understanding decisions. Confidence intervals overlap, so read this as tiers, not exact ranks. The order is unchanged without straight-liners. Among anonymous respondents recognition rises into the top tier and cross-team work drops out. These are associations, not causes.

**Evidence:** priority_matrix.csv; driver_model_summary.csv; notebook Step 7.1. Pride and belonging excluded as near-outcomes.

## 07 · Two themes are backed by all four sources of evidence

We checked every candidate theme against four independent sources: how low its questions score, how strongly they link to recommending, satisfaction and intention to stay, how many people chose it as the one thing HR should improve, and how many raise it when asked 'if you could make one change' (the only question that names no topic). Grading rules were fixed before looking at the result. Fair decisions with growth, and leaders listening and acting, are strong on all four. Recognition scores lowest and is raised often. Cross-team work links to outcomes but is mid-table on score and rarely raised unprompted. Pay and facilities have no rating question but are a clear HR request, so they are routed to the annual review rather than ignored.

**Evidence:** priority_triangulation.csv; priority_matrix.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv; one_change_topics.csv; grading rules fixed in notebook Step 7.3.

## 08 · Make people decisions fair, explainable and visible

The five lowest questions in the survey all sit here: having efforts recognised, celebrating successes, decision consistency, understanding how decisions are made, and fair rewards and recognition. Equal growth opportunities scores higher but is one of the two strongest actionable links to outcomes. Fairness is the block most strongly linked to recommending, satisfaction and intention to stay (31% of the explained variation), and 51 people chose career progression as the one thing HR should improve. Recognition belongs here: the fairness question itself asks whether recognition and rewards are fair, and recognition weighs more heavily among anonymous respondents. The 137 people with at least one fairness answer of 1–3 include 'Sometimes', so this is breadth, not the rate of unfair treatment. The quote is one verbatim, de-identified comment chosen because it is typical of the fairness answers.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv; driver_model_summary.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv; fairness_response_breadth.csv; quote verbatim, de-identified.

## 09 · Leaders listen, and visibly act on what they hear

Asked to pick the one thing HR should do better, 67 people chose listening to employees and their concerns, the most common answer. Another 13 chose keeping employees informed. 'Leaders are willing to listen to employee feedback and act on it' is 79.8% favourable and is the question most strongly linked to recommending, satisfaction and staying among the actionable ones. In the open 'one change' question, 97 people ask for better communication or follow-through, more than for any other theme. The ask is not more announcements; it is evidence that raising something leads to a response. Benefits and facilities come joint second in the HR question and are addressed on slide 12.

**Evidence:** hr_priority_counts.csv; priority_matrix.csv (CRE5); priority_triangulation.csv; quote verbatim, de-identified.

## 10 · Start where the gaps are largest: Customer Service and 3–5-year staff

Customer Service is lowest or close to lowest on every priority column: recognition 57%, fair decisions 64% and intention to stay 76%. The 3–5-year group dips on fair decisions, recognition and intention to stay. Women are 9–13 points below men on fair decisions, growth, listening and recognition. Use this to choose where to pilot and listen first, not to rank or blame teams. Groups overlap, and composition or role mix could explain part of each pattern.

**Evidence:** group_hotspots.csv; notebook Step 7.5. Smaller departments and the mixed 'Other' group not shown.

## 11 · Cross-team handovers: a real but narrower gap to fix where it bites

Own-team support is 91.1% favourable versus 82.3% for cross-team collaboration. The paired table is the stronger evidence: 45 people rate their own team 4–5 but cross-team work 1–3, while only 16 show the reverse, which is unlikely to be chance. But cross-team collaboration ranks 9th of 30 questions, only 16 people raise teamwork in the open 'one change' question, and among anonymous respondents it drops out of the top tier of outcome links. So it is a targeted operational fix rather than the headline. Customer Service is lowest at 75%, so start there, and verify one real handover with both sides before designing anything.

**Evidence:** cross_team_deep_dive.csv; cross_team_paired_detail.csv; group_hotspots.csv; CAM1 × CAM2; n = 327.

## 12 · In their own words, people ask for communication, recognition and growth

The open 'one change' question is the cleanest comment evidence, because it does not steer people towards a topic. Communication and decision explanations, recognition, career growth and follow-through lead; teamwork is raised by only 16 people. Pay, benefits and facilities matter too: 51 people chose 'improve employee benefits and facilities' as HR's one fix, and 37 raise pay or facilities here. These are outside a 90-day pilot, so say publicly that they go to the annual compensation and facilities review. Finally, fix the survey itself: drop the name field and collect headcount so a response rate can be reported.

**Evidence:** one_change_topics.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv; priority_triangulation.csv; named_vs_anonymous.csv.

## 13 · One 90-day pilot, three workstreams, clear accountability

Priority 1 is owned by the HR head with business heads: publish the criteria for appraisal, promotion and reward in the pilot roles, record and share the reason for each decision with a route to ask for review, and have managers give each person specific recognition every month. Priority 2 is owned by a leadership sponsor with HR: log every raised concern with an owner and a next-update date, publish a monthly 'you said, we did' update, and hold skip-level listening sessions starting in Customer Service. The handover pilot is owned by the Operations head with both team leads. All owners are proposals. No new platform is assumed. Specific accounts about facilities, respectful treatment or working-time boundaries need confidential verification through existing routes.

**Evidence:** implementation_roadmap_0_90_days.csv (adapted to the revised priorities); proposed actions, not approved commitments.

## 14 · A 90-day pilot turns the findings into a decision

Day 0 is organisational approval of scope, sponsor, owners and capacity. In the first 14 days, choose the pilot area (Customer Service first), review existing criteria and run an anonymous baseline pulse on the priority questions. Days 15–30 publish criteria, assign owners, open the concern-to-action log and agree the handover checklist. Days 31–60 run monthly recognition and updates, review handover exceptions weekly, and check burden and usefulness. Days 61–90 repeat the anonymous pulse, compare process measures, confirm pride and belonging held, and decide whether to scale, adapt or stop.

**Evidence:** implementation_roadmap_0_90_days.csv; roadmap_decision_gates.csv (adapted to the revised priorities); all standards and owners are proposals.

## 15 · Measure the process and whether employees notice

Each workstream has a process check and an experience check. The process checks are design standards, not survey predictions. The experience checks repeat the exact survey questions in an anonymous pulse, so results are comparable and less affected by the name field. The +5-point figures are indicative and should be confirmed at Day 14 once the pilot baseline is known; a before-and-after change alone does not prove the pilot caused it. Guardrails make sure the pilot does not erode pride, own-team support or intention to stay.

**Evidence:** roadmap_kpi_dictionary.csv; item_evidence_profile.csv; group_hotspots.csv; named_vs_anonymous.csv. Targets are indicative.

## 16 · Three decisions to start on Monday

Close with three specific decisions. First, approve one 90-day pilot covering fair decisions and recognition, the listen-and-act loop, and one Customer Service handover. Second, name the owners (a leadership sponsor, the HR head and the Operations head) and protect their time with a monthly review. Third, run the next survey anonymously: remove the name field, run a baseline pulse in the first 14 days and repeat it at Day 90. Invite discussion on which pilot roles and which handover matter most to verify first.

**Evidence:** FINAL_ANALYSIS_SYNTHESIS.md; implementation_roadmap_0_90_days.csv; roadmap_decision_gates.csv; notebook Step 7.6.

## 17 · Methods were selected for the decision

Primary evidence is counts, distributions, favourable shares and paired comparisons. Working means assume equal category spacing and weights. Step 7 adds relative-weights analysis on 19 actionable questions (pride and belonging excluded as near-outcomes) with 500 bootstrap resamples, and a four-source evidence check with grading rules fixed before reading the result. Comment analysis uses 15 explicit overlapping topics with stance, assisted by AI and re-reviewed once; it is not independently validated. Exact bigrams and trigrams help navigate phrases. The topic network describes same-answer co-mentions and is prompt-sensitive. Sensitivity checks exclude straight-liners and use anonymous respondents only. None of these establishes causation.

**Evidence:** ANALYSIS_FROM_DATA_TO_ACTION.md §12; notebooks/00_data_understanding_eda.ipynb, Step 7 and final methodology cell.

## 18 · The priorities hold up under the main robustness checks

Favourable shares fall when straight-liners are excluded and fall further among anonymous respondents, but the ordering of strengths and gaps is the same: pride stays high, and fairness, recognition and listening stay lowest. The driver ranking is identical without straight-liners (Spearman 1.00). Among the 100 anonymous respondents it shifts (Spearman 0.59): growth, fair rewards and leaders acting on feedback stay in the top tier, recognition rises into it and cross-team work drops out. That small group points the same way as the evidence check. The comment re-review covered 568 comments and changed 53 decisions; 75 cases still need context, and no independent coder was available.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv; priority_matrix.csv; named_vs_anonymous.csv; coding_review_before_after.csv; assistant_review_status.csv.

## 19 · Use group patterns to guide listening, not to assign blame

The 3–5-year tenure group and Customer Service are examples for further listening, not a ranking of problem groups. Eleven of 53 responses in the 3–5-year group show favourable own-team support and a 1–3 cross-team rating; 27 have at least one fairness item at 1–3. Corresponding Customer Service counts are 10 of 51 and 29 of 51. Departments and tenure groups overlap, so they are not independent comparisons. Composition, selection, role mix and working context could explain patterns. The heterogeneous Other grouping is unsuitable for a simple team-level diagnosis. Cross-team and fairness patterns overlap for 47 records but 90 records have a fairness item at 1–3 despite favourable cross-team collaboration, which is why the two themes should not be collapsed into one explanation.

**Evidence:** focused_group_profile.csv; cross_team_fairness_overlap.csv; whole-study n = 327; selected groups n ≥ 10.

## 20 · The topic map helps organise discussion

The full primary network includes 1,230 topic-coded suggestion/mixed answers from 305 response records, 13 displayed topics and 34 links supported by at least ten answers. This slide shows a selected five-topic excerpt using original node support and same-answer counts; positions are schematic, not analytical distances. Communication and teamwork co-occur in 98 answers from 93 records; 85 of those answers arise from the teamwork prompt. Fairness and career co-occur in 46 answers from 43 records; 41 come from the fairness prompt. Prompt dependence and parameter sensitivity limit any claim that the graph independently validates a mechanism. Node sizes represent unique-record support within the primary network context, line widths represent answer co-mentions. The offline interactive map provides question exclusion and further context.

**Evidence:** topic_network_edges.csv; topic_network_parameter_sensitivity.csv; reports/network/topic_network_data.json.
