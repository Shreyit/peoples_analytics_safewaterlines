# Presentation storyline and speaker guide

A 19-slide, 20-minute pitch in plain English. Each slide's notes start with its time window; read them as a script or in your own words.

Present from Safewater_Lines_Pitch_20min.pptx (no internal file references). The TEAM_with_sources copy shows the evidence file behind each slide.

## 01 · What our people are telling us

[0:00 – 0:30] Good morning. We analysed the Your Voice survey: 327 people, 30 questions and almost 1,900 written comments. In the next 20 minutes we will tell you what employees value, what they want fixed first, and a simple 90-day plan to start.

**Evidence:** Your Voice survey (responses to 21 October); analysis 30 October 2026.

## 02 · Three things to remember

[0:30 – 1:30] Here is the whole story in one slide. One: people are proud of this company and they help each other. That is a real strength. Two: they want people decisions, like promotions, pay rises and recognition, to be fair and clear. These are the lowest scores in the survey. Three: they want leaders to listen and then act. It was the number one thing people asked HR to do better. Our plan is one 90-day pilot, starting in Customer Service.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv; priority_triangulation.csv.

## 03 · Who took part

[1:30 – 2:30] 327 people answered. They could reply in English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or Malayalam, from 23 locations, and they wrote almost 1,900 comments. Mumbai is the biggest group, about 40% of answers, and 38% have been here less than 3 years. One limit: we were not given the total number of employees, so we cannot say what share of staff took part.

**Evidence:** survey_clean.csv; workbook language sheets.

## 04 · How to read the numbers

[2:30 – 3:00] Every question had five possible answers, from Almost never to Almost always. When we say a number like 80% positive, it means 8 out of 10 people answered Usually or Almost always. Sometimes is the middle answer, so we do not count it as positive. That is all you need to read the rest of the slides.

**Evidence:** Survey answer scale; favourable = 4–5.

## 05 · The good news: people are proud and they help each other

[3:00 – 4:00] Let's start with the good news, because it is big. Almost everyone understands how their work helps the business. 92% are proud to work here. 91% say people in their team help each other, and 90% feel they belong. When we asked what people never want to lose, 119 people named their team or colleagues. Whatever we change, we must protect this.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv (PRI2, PRI1, CAM1, CAM3).

## 06 · One warning: answers with a name look more positive

[4:00 – 5:30] One important warning. The survey asked people for their name, and 69% gave it. People who stayed anonymous were clearly less positive. For example, 92% of named people plan to stay 3 to 5 years, but only 78% of anonymous people. People may be more careful when their name is attached. So the real picture is probably a little less rosy than the headline numbers. Our simple advice: do not ask for names next time.

**Evidence:** named_vs_anonymous.csv; notebook Step 7.4.

## 07 · Where it hurts: the five lowest scores

[5:30 – 6:30] Now the gaps. These are the five lowest scores out of 30 questions. Look at what they have in common: recognition, consistent decisions, understanding how decisions are made, and fair rewards. All of them are about how people decisions are made and explained. They are about 10 points below the average of all questions.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv (RES4, CAM5, FAI3, FAI5, FAI1).

## 08 · How we decided what to fix first

[6:30 – 8:00] Low scores alone are not enough to choose priorities, so we checked every theme in four ways. One: is the score low? Two: does it move together with people wanting to stay and recommend the company? Three: did people pick it when asked what HR should do better? Four: did people raise it on their own when we asked for one change? We set the pass marks before looking at the results.

**Evidence:** Notebook Step 7.3; pass marks fixed before reading the result.

## 09 · Two themes pass all four tests

[8:00 – 9:00] Here is the result. Only two themes are strong on all four tests: fair people decisions with career growth, and leaders who listen and act. Those are our two priorities. Recognition scores lowest and people write about it a lot, so we put it inside priority one. Teamwork across teams is real but smaller, so it gets a targeted fix. Pay and facilities matter to people but there was no survey question on them, so we send them to the yearly review.

**Evidence:** priority_triangulation.csv; notebook Step 7.3.

## 10 · Priority 1: fair decisions that people understand

[9:00 – 10:30] Priority one is fair decisions that people understand. Only 75% say people decisions are made consistently. Of the areas leaders can change, fairness is the one most linked to people wanting to stay and recommend the company. And 51 people said HR's main job should be helping careers grow. In one employee's words: inconsistent performance evaluations and opaque promotion criteria. What it means in practice: clear rules for promotion, pay and rewards, and a reason given for every decision.

**Evidence:** item_evidence_profile.csv; driver_model_summary.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv; quote verbatim, de-identified.

## 11 · Priority 2: leaders who listen, and then act

[10:30 – 12:00] Priority two is leaders who listen and then act. We asked everyone to pick the one thing HR should do better. The top answer, by a clear margin, was listening to employees and their concerns: 67 people. Today only 80% say leaders act on feedback, and among the actionable questions it is the one most linked to people wanting to stay. People are not asking for more announcements. They want proof that when they raise something, something happens.

**Evidence:** hr_priority_counts.csv; item_evidence_profile.csv (CRE5); quote verbatim, de-identified.

## 12 · Where to start: Customer Service

[12:00 – 13:00] Where should we start? This table shows the share of positive answers by group, and darker means lower. Customer Service is the lowest group on almost everything: only 57% feel recognised, and 76% plan to stay. People with 3 to 5 years' service also dip. Women answer less positively than men on fairness and recognition. This is not about blaming a team. It tells us where to listen first and where a pilot can make the biggest difference.

**Evidence:** group_hotspots.csv; notebook Step 7.5.

## 13 · A smaller fix: handovers between teams

[13:00 – 14:00] There is one smaller issue. People rate their own team very highly, 91%, but teams working together across the company lower, 82%. 45 people rate their own team well but other teams poorly. It is real, but smaller than the two priorities, and people rarely raised it on their own. So we suggest a targeted fix: take one handover in Customer Service and agree who sends what, who receives it, and who covers when someone is away.

**Evidence:** cross_team_paired_detail.csv; item_evidence_profile.csv (CAM1, CAM2).

## 14 · What else people told us

[14:00 – 15:00] When we asked an open question, if you could make one change, people mostly asked for better communication, recognition, career growth and follow-through. That matches our two priorities. People also care about pay, benefits and facilities: 51 people picked it as HR's main job. A 90-day pilot cannot fix pay, so we suggest sending it to the yearly pay and facilities review, and telling people clearly that this is where it went.

**Evidence:** one_change_topics.csv; hr_priority_counts.csv.

## 15 · Our plan: one 90-day pilot, three parts

[15:00 – 16:30] Here is the plan. One 90-day pilot with three parts, starting in Customer Service. Part one, owned by the HR head: publish clear rules for promotion, pay and rewards, give a reason for every decision, and have managers recognise each person every month. Part two, owned by a leadership sponsor: log every concern with an owner and a date, and send a monthly 'you said, we did' update. Part three, owned by the Operations head: fix one handover between teams. None of this needs a new system.

**Evidence:** implementation_roadmap_0_90_days.csv (revised IDs P1, P2, T); proposed owners, not approved commitments.

## 16 · 90 days, step by step

[16:30 – 17:30] Step by step. In the first two weeks we choose the pilot teams, check today's rules and run a short anonymous survey as our starting point. By day 30 the rules are published, owners are named and the concern log is open. From day 31 to 60 we run it, review problems every week and check it is not adding paperwork. In the last month we repeat the short survey and decide together: grow it, change it or stop it.

**Evidence:** roadmap_decision_gates.csv (adapted).

## 17 · How we will know it worked

[17:30 – 18:30] How will we know it worked? For each part we check two things: did we do the work, and did employees notice? For example, rules published for every pilot role, and the share who say decisions are consistent going up from 75% by about 5 points. We measure with a short anonymous survey so people can be honest. And we watch our strengths, pride and teamwork, to make sure they do not drop.

**Evidence:** roadmap_kpi_dictionary.csv; item_evidence_profile.csv. Targets are suggestions to confirm in the first 14 days.

## 18 · What we ask you to decide today

[18:30 – 19:30] We are asking for three decisions today. One: approve one 90-day pilot with the three parts. Two: name the owners, the HR head, a leadership sponsor and the Operations head, and give them time for one review a month. Three: run the next survey without names. Thank you. We are happy to take questions.

**Evidence:** implementation_roadmap_0_90_days.csv; roadmap_decision_gates.csv.

## 19 · Thank you. Questions?

[19:30 – 20:00] Thank you. Before questions, three honest limits. These links are not proof of cause and effect. We were not given the total headcount. And the comments were read with AI help and checked once, not by an independent team. We are happy to take questions, and we can show any result by department or tenure on the dashboard.

**Evidence:** Safewater_Lines_Full_Reference.pdf; dashboard/dist/index.html.
